## The headline

These are not 15 apps with their own problems. They are one
codebase, forked 23 times, with the same
handful of defects in every copy. Sorted by how many apps carry each one:

| Defect | Apps | Fixes | Where the fix belongs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Print output defects | **15** | 67 | Half these apps exist to produce paper. Printing is a feature, not an afterthought. |
| Silent caps and truncation | **15** | 54 | The single most repeated defect. Every limit must say its own name at the moment it bites — a cap discovered later is the category betrayal the research names. |
| Focus destroyed on redraw | **15** | 29 | Apps that rebuild their DOM drop focus to <body> on every interaction. Preserve focus and selection across a redraw, keyed on a stable identity. |
| No undo on destructive actions | **15** | 27 | SWS.undo() in the shared runtime. Undo, not a confirm dialog: a confirm taxes the 99 deliberate taps to catch the one mistake, and is dismissed on reflex. |
| Targets under the 44px floor | **15** | 27 | --tap in the base is the floor and scales with the comfort panel. App layers still set their own smaller paddings. |
| Breaks on a narrow screen | **14** | 25 | Measured by the stress battery at 320px, 414px, and at largest text and roomy spacing. |
| Contrast and colour-only state | **13** | 28 | The build contrast-solves every token. Failures are app layers reaching past the tokens, in states that only exist after interaction — which is why axe misses them. |
| Comfort panel ignored | **13** | 28 | Canvas and animation get no cascade. They have to read the root font size and honour data-motion themselves. |
| Never says the work is saved | **13** | 19 | SWS.saved(). Apps speak only when saving FAILS, so the evidence that a list survived is that nothing happened. |
| Privacy promise as grey copy | **11** | 25 | The .trust component. Research is blunt: nobody believes a privacy claim written as body text at the bottom of a page. |
| Unlabelled controls | **7** | 13 | A placeholder is not a label: it disappears exactly when the user needs it, mid-form. |
| No empty state | **7** | 7 | An empty state is the cheapest onboarding there is, and most apps open cold showing an empty box. |

The practical consequence: anything at the top of that table should be fixed
**once** — in `design/studio.css`, in the shared runtime, or by a sweep over
all 23 app layers — and never as 15 separate tasks. Anything at
the bottom is genuinely per-app work.

### Why our own tooling missed most of this

axe reports **zero violations** across all 23 apps in both themes, and the
contrast build passes 1542/1542. Both are true, and both missed the defects
above, for the same reason: **they scan the page as it loads.** Almost every
real failure here lives in a state that does not exist yet —
`.active`, `.sel`, `.winner`, a list with 200 rows in it, a canvas
repainted after a preference change, a PDF that has not been generated.

That is what `design/stress.mjs` exists for, and why it runs axe *after*
interacting rather than on load.

## What the incumbents are hated for

Counted across 23 competitor studies. This is the market
position stated as evidence rather than as a slogan:

| Complaint | Instances | Categories |
|---|---|---|
| Other | 111 | 23 |
| Subscription or paywall | 48 | 21 |
| Uploads and privacy worry | 34 | 17 |
| Forced account | 34 | 18 |
| Export, print or watermark | 24 | 13 |
| Ads and upsell in the workflow | 17 | 12 |
| Loses work, slow or crashes | 12 | 8 |
| Bloat and too many taps | 7 | 5 |

Every one of those companies has a payroll, and that payroll is why they must
ask for an account, run an ad, or put the export behind a card. We do not.
**The promise is the product** — which is exactly why leaving it as grey
footer copy in 21 of 23 apps is a strategic error and not a cosmetic one.

## What people asked for and did not find

Straight from the focus groups' `mostWantedNext`. These are features, not
defects — the backlog after the bleeding stops.

**baby-log**
  - Make Undo actually deliverable: keep it alive across subsequent toasts (queue it, or park it as an inline row affordance rather than in the toast), and focus it after a frame so the visibility transition has settled.
  - Per-event writes and a cross-tab guard — append to an events store instead of overwriting one blob, and listen on BroadcastChannel or the storage event so a second tab reconciles instead of clobbering.
  - Show every entry: page the timeline over the whole log instead of over the first 200, and say how many are hidden rather than hiding the button.
  - Backdating and edit, done right — tap a row to adjust its time, with times entered after midnight resolving to the nearest past occurrence and displayed as 'Yesterday 10:00 PM' in words. The research says publish this as a feature.
  - A 'still going?' affordance on any sleep past ~5 hours or feed past ~60 minutes, with a one-tap 'ended at ___', never a modal.
  - A night-aware day boundary — let the day roll at 05:00, or offer 'last 24 hours' alongside 'today' — so the handoff summary matches the shift it describes.
  - Per-day totals on the printed sheet: seven lines of feeds / wet / dirty / sleep above the detail rows, and naps printed as durations rather than as start and stop rows.
  - Clamp and sanity-check timestamps on import and on display, and guard agoText against negative intervals.

**bracket-maker**
  - Preserve results when the entrant list is edited — match by identity, not by position — and put the wipe behind SWS.undo if it must ever happen at all.
  - Reset the palette tokens inside the app's @media print block, mark winners structurally rather than with a background fill, and add a .printonly header carrying the event title and date.
  - Make the title real: render it above the bracket, into document.title, and onto the printed sheet.
  - Either tell the truth about the link ('Copy a snapshot — re-copy after each result') or make it live via the portfolio's share-doc pattern, which is the research's single highest-value differentiator.
  - Give the bracket semantics and keep focus: aria-pressed on the two sides of a match, a role and name on each pairing, real headings for round names, non-interactive elements for byes, and focus restored to the equivalent node after re-render.
  - Drop overflow:hidden from .match and round the child buttons individually, so the shared focus ring is visible on the control the app is made of.
  - Anchor each round label to its own matches, draw connectors between rounds, and give the scroller a fade plus scroll-snap — or a fit-to-screen overview, which is what the research says wins this category.
  - Show the URL as selectable text with a copy button inside the QR dialog, shorten the payload (or fall back to a short share link) so the code stays scannable, and give the dialog a real accessible name and dark-mode colours.
  - A live entrant count with a warning when the 32-name cap or the 40-character truncation bites.
  - navigator.share on the share button, falling back to clipboard.

**caregiver-log**
  - A real medication action: pick the drug, show 'last given 2:05pm by Rosalie' in large type before the save, and require an explicit confirm inside the interval. Nothing else in this app is worth more.
  - A when field on every entry — defaulting to now, one tap to change — and inline edit of the body and the time, forever, for whoever wrote it and the owner.
  - 'Print the last 7 days' as the default print action, with a standing reference block (medications and doses, allergies, doctors, pharmacy, where the directive lives) and the collected open questions printed at the top.
  - Take the tip jar and the 'Start yours' footer off any board the viewer does not own, and put the ask in the owner's settings panel only.
  - Fix the print palette reset to match the dark selector's specificity, in the shared base — this is a one-line change that affects all 23 apps and lands hardest here.
  - Make Undo reachable: move focus into the toast when it carries an action, give each pending delete its own Undo instead of one shared toast slot, and keep the affordance until it is dismissed.
  - A 'last updated N minutes ago by <name>' line at the top of the board and a locally-computed 'new since you last looked' divider in the timeline.
  - Idempotent week-adding (skip labels already present, and say so), no 100-day cap, and a way to archive a past week in one action instead of seven dialogs.
  - Browser-native dictation on the composer, and a JSON/text export plus import so a year of a family's log is not one 'Delete log' away from gone.
  - Scale the type picker with --tap and the text-size scale, and give the coverage row a stable place for the edit control that is not adjacent to 'I can be there'.

**grocery-list**
  - Keep checked items where they are. Fade them in place, or move them into a separate collapsed 'In the cart (6)' group below a divider — never re-sort the live list under a finger.
  - Make the add field survive its own app: give it a data-fkey so the existing focus-restore path covers it, restore the caret, and never let a household member's snapshot close someone else's keyboard.
  - Undo on every destructive action via the SWS.undo already sitting in the page — item delete first, Clear checked second — and drop the confirm() dialogs once undo exists.
  - Quantity and a short note per row without opening a modal, and dedupe on normalized name at add-time (bump or flash the existing row).
  - Aisle sections with a user override that sticks, and drag-to-reorder the sections into one store's walking route.
  - An honest saved/offline state: a persistent 'Saved on this phone' badge, a distinct offline message, and a resolveCode failure that says 'you look offline' instead of accusing the user of mistyping.
  - 'Copy list as text' beside Copy link and QR, for the household member who will only ever use SMS.
  - Show the list's name on the board, on the print, and in the share panel.

**pill-schedule**
  - A live preview of the actual sheet on screen, with an honest page-fit meter — 'fits on one page' / '19 rows: this will print on 2 pages'.
  - Output type-size presets at 18 / 22 / 28pt, wired to the printed card rather than to the operator's screen, with the fit meter reacting to them.
  - Edit a row in place, and a visible 'x of 30 rows' counter that warns before it refuses.
  - An allergies block, prescriber and pharmacy phone numbers, and a separate as-needed section — the table stakes every clinical form has and this card does not.
  - A printed-on date on the card, and the name repeated on every page.
  - A duplicate-name flag before printing, worded as 'double-check with your pharmacist'.
  - Export and re-import a plain file, so the promise that the data is theirs is something they can hold.

**scan-to-pdf**
  - Persist the session — pages into IndexedDB as blobs on every add, restored on load with a 'you have 8 pages from earlier, keep or clear?' prompt. This is the difference between a demo and a tool.
  - Refuse a page you cannot embed, at the moment it is added: decode-test each file, reject with a named error ('scan.heic — this browser can't read HEIC photos, try exporting as JPEG'), and never let the export ship a blank page while saying 'saved'.
  - Tap a thumbnail to see the whole page, full-bleed, with rotate / retake / delete on that view — and stop cover-cropping the grid.
  - Undo on delete via the SWS.undo that is already loaded, plus a visible 'Clear everything' that says out loud that nothing was stored.
  - Live capture feedback: render each page as it finishes rather than after the batch, put aria-live on the count, and show a 'Page 7 added' flash.
  - Output control under the export button — editable filename with a date and a label chip, a live size estimate, a quality/dpi choice, and fit-to-image page sizing for landscape captures.
  - Grayscale and hard black-and-white filters. A canvas one-liner each, they halve the file size, they are the most-requested thing after edge detection, and they make an office-lit receipt legible.
  - Make the four page controls real 44px targets that scale with --ui-scale, name them by page ('Move page 3 earlier'), and keep focus on the equivalent control after a redraw.

**seating-chart**
  - Undo on every destructive action, replacing all five confirm() calls — the shared SWS.undo runtime is already loaded and unused.
  - A persistent unseated tray with a live count that is visible from every tab, not a heading inside one card.
  - 'Changed since last export' diffing, so the 72-hour churn means reprinting six cards instead of 140.
  - Keyboard seating: focusable table cards, a 'move to table' action, and focus preserved across redraws.
  - A guest search inside the Add-a-rule dialog, and a way to see which table a searched guest is at from the Guests tab.
  - A scrollable or zoomable floor plan that grows with the table count, plus non-table objects (bar, dance floor, DJ) and tables above 30 seats.
  - Unicode-capable PDF output — embed a subsetted TrueType face instead of the Standard-14 fonts — so a guest's actual name appears on their card.
  - Read-only share or a printable alphabetical guest→table sign as a paper fallback, for the coordinator, caterer and venue who all need the list and none of whom should need an account.

**secret-santa**
  - Persist the group, the pairs and the draw in localStorage, and let the organizer reopen tomorrow, finish sending, and reuse the roster next year — the research names this as a feature two competitors charge money for.
  - Make redrawing an explicit, guarded act, and after any redraw show which people's links are unchanged and which need re-sending. This is the research's #5 differentiator and it is the same fix as the latecomer case.
  - A verification line under the results: 'N people · everyone gives once · everyone receives once · nobody drew themselves · N no-match rules respected' — the correctness already exists and is invisible.
  - A 'hide again' control on the reveal, and lift the name to 2.5–3rem.
  - A real error state for a broken reveal link: 'This link is incomplete — ask whoever sent it to send it again.'
  - Sent-state on each link row, so a 22-person send can survive an interruption.
  - Paper Mode — printable fold-over slips — which the research calls out as the one thing no digital competitor offers, and which the current print path is a long way from.

**signup-sheets**
  - Stop rebuilding the world: keep the Manage panel's open state and the addMode across redraws, and patch the slot list instead of replacing <main> — that one change fixes the collapsing panel, the wiped note field, the lost focus and most of the screen-reader noise at once.
  - Report what was dropped. 'Added 100 — 50 more lines were not imported' and never clear a textarea whose contents were not accepted.
  - Raise 'Claim this spot' and the release ✕ to var(--tap), and give the release a word ('Give it back') rather than a 16px glyph.
  - Hold the claim dialog open until the write resolves, show progress on the button, and on a collision keep the note, say 'that one just filled', and offer the nearest open spot as one tap.
  - Per-person claim cap in Settings ('anyone may take up to N'), which the rules layer can enforce the same way it enforces capacity.
  - Undo via SWS.undo on all four confirm() paths, plus a note to the affected participants when a spot they hold is deleted.
  - Either finish the theme system (drive --accent-fill, --accent-deep, --focus and --accent-soft from the choice, add a 'default' option) or delete it, which is what the research recommends.
  - An 'open spots only' toggle and a still-needed sentence at the top — 'Still needed: 3 salads, 2 desserts, someone to bring ice' — instead of only a progress bar.
  - Put the share code and a QR on the printed sheet, drop the 'you run this' badge and the empty-state lemonade line from print, and add a date.
  - An .ics download at the moment of claiming, the research's named answer to reminder emails and currently the most visible gap in the feature set.
  - Let people edit a claim, not just delete it — renameClaim() is already written and tested at the data layer.

**sitter-sheet**
  - A read-only sitter view rendered from the same data, opened automatically when the URL carries a sheet, led by a 3-second panel: street address + cross street, allergies and medications, and 911 / Poison Control 1-800-222-1222 — all as 48px tel: targets at 22px+.
  - Stop rendering placeholders as content: drop them from any hash-loaded sheet, and give the parent's form a visible filled/empty cue that survives High contrast.
  - A named completeness nudge — 'No allergy info. No backup contact. No cross street.' — shown before Print and before Copy link, silent about the other sixty fields.
  - Make the QR path honest: retry at lower ECC, size modules to at least 4px, and when the payload genuinely will not fit, say so and offer Copy link and Copy as text instead of a dead toast.
  - 'Copy as text' as the third equal button, and a 'Download my sheet' JSON export with a matching import — the direct, nameable answer to the Cozi lock-in complaint.
  - A live print preview on screen that is literally the printed page, plus a fix for the two-column pagination so long answers never lose their tail.
  - Per-child (and per-pet) repeatable blocks, and an optional collapsed consent-to-treat + insurance section with a signature line.

**specials-planner**
  - Key lessons to cycle position, not to the date — plan[letter][period] with the calendar as a rendered projection — and add the one-tap bump with an undo toast. This is the app's reason to exist and everything else in the category is downstream of it.
  - A class/section layer: name the class in each period slot ('3rd — Alvarez'), colour by grade, and let a lesson be authored once and assigned to every section of a grade, with a per-section 'where we left off' line.
  - A Now/Next hallway card as the phone default: the class walking in, the lesson, the materials line, one thumb, nothing else on screen.
  - Undo on every destructive action, using the SWS.undo runtime that is already loaded — the two fill buttons, restore-from-backup, and erase.
  - Fix the grid's typography and colour so the comfort panel reaches it: tokens instead of the eight hardcoded px sizes and the #fff / #EFE6D2 / #E9E2D2 literals.
  - A one-week-on-one-page print mode with a compact/full toggle and letter/legal choice, plus a direct PDF button from the week view.
  - A save you can trust: pagehide flush, a visible timestamped 'saved 12:41' rather than a fading pill, and a loud, actionable warning the moment a write fails.

## How to work this list

1. **Sweeps first.** Every row in the headline table touching 8+ apps is a
   sweep or a base-layer change. Doing those as per-app tasks is the single
   biggest waste available.
2. **Then blockers, worst app first**, using the per-app `fixes` array in
   `<slug>.review.json` — already ordered, already located, already
   evidenced.
3. **Verify by measurement.** `node design/stress.mjs <slug>` must end at
   zero hard failures and `node design/a11y.mjs <slug>` at zero violations.
   A fix without a number next to it is an intention.
