Privacy and accessibility

Comic Crew is a comic book maker for children, made by Sky Wolf Studio. A faint blue figure waits under the paper, a child traces it, inks it, dresses it, poses it and prints a page. Here is the short version, which is also the whole version: the drawings stay in this browser, on this device, and nothing is uploaded.

The whole thing, in six lines

  • Drawings, characters and comic pages are kept in this browser's storage on this device. Nothing is uploaded.
  • There is no account and no sign in. Nothing is asked for: not a name, not an email address, not an age.
  • No analytics, no ad network, no trackers, no third party code of any kind.
  • The only way anything leaves this device is a person choosing Save, Send or Print.
  • The web server that hands you the page records an IP address, so the page can be delivered and the server defended. It is not used to build a profile of anyone.
  • Browsers clear their own storage without asking. If a comic matters, print it or save it as a file the same day.

Where the drawings go

Nowhere. Every line, every character, every speech balloon and every comic panel is written to this browser's own storage on this device, under a single name, comic-crew.character, which holds the character's name, the lines that were drawn, the pose and the panels. That is the entire list.

There is no account, no sign in, no server to sync to, and no copy held anywhere we can reach. We could not hand a child's drawings to anyone if we were asked for them, because we do not have them.

The one warning a parent actually needs

Browsers clear their own storage, and they do not ask first. Safari deletes storage that a page created after seven days without a visit. Clearing history or site data clears it. A school or guest Chromebook session usually wipes everything at logout. Comic Crew asks the browser to keep its storage anyway, but that is a request, not a promise, and no app can make it one.

So if a comic matters, get it off the browser the day it is made. Any of these does it, and they take about a minute:

  • Print it from the finished page. Paper is the only copy that no browser can delete.
  • Save picture writes the page as a .png to this device.
  • Save to a file in the Grown ups panel writes the character as a small .json file you can keep, back up, or open again on another device.

A seven year old will not understand why a drawing vanished. Saving it now is the whole fix.

The only ways anything leaves this device

Four, and every one of them starts with a person deliberately tapping a button. Nothing happens on its own, and none of them uploads anything to us.

  • Print it hands the finished page to your own device's print dialog, and from there to your own printer.
  • Save picture writes a .png of the page to your own downloads or photos.
  • Save to a file writes a .json character file to your own downloads. It is the only backup that exists, so it is worth making.
  • Send it passes the picture to your device's own share sheet, and you pick who receives it. We never see the picture and we keep no record of who it went to.

Opening a saved character file works the same way in reverse: your browser's file picker hands the app that one file, and the app can see nothing else on the device.

What Comic Crew does not do

  • No account, no sign in, no name, no email address, no age and no birthday. Nothing is asked for, so nothing can be given.
  • No analytics, no ad network, no advertising trackers, no cookies, no fingerprinting.
  • No third party code of any kind. No script, font or image is fetched from anybody: both typefaces ship inside the app.
  • The one exception, and it is a link rather than code: if a tip button is showing in the Grown ups sheet, tapping it opens a Stripe checkout page in a new tab, and from that point Stripe's own privacy policy applies. Nothing about the child or the drawing goes with it, and we never see or store card details. Nothing is sent to Stripe unless a grown up taps that button.
  • No chat, no comments, no gallery, no upload, no public profile. There is no way for a child to be contacted by anyone through this app, because there is no channel to contact them on.
  • No purchases inside the app, no paywall, no trial, no watermark and no export limit.
  • Nothing is sold, shared or brokered. There is no data here to sell.

The one record a visit does create

Serving a web page needs a server. The one that hands you Comic Crew is Google's Firebase Hosting, and like every web host it writes an ordinary, short lived log line when a browser asks for a file. That line includes the IP address the request came from. It exists so the page can be delivered and so the host can deal with attacks and abuse.

It is not used to build a profile of anyone. It is not combined with anything else, it is not sold, and it cannot contain a single drawing, because drawings are never sent to it. We say it plainly because it is the only piece of information a visit creates at all, and a page about an app for children should not leave anything out.

Deleting everything

There is nothing on our side to delete, because nothing is kept on our side. On the device:

  • New character in the Grown ups panel clears the character and starts over. Undo brings it back if that was a mistake.
  • Clearing this site's data in your browser settings removes everything Comic Crew has stored, including its offline copy of the app itself.
  • Removing the app from the home screen does the same on most devices.

Files that were already saved or printed are yours, in your own downloads or on your own paper, and nothing here can reach them.

Permissions

None. Comic Crew asks for no camera, no microphone, no location, no contacts, no notifications and no access to the photo library. Save picture and Send it go through your device's own download and share sheet, which you drive, and the file picker only ever hands over the one file you choose.

Children

This app is made for children, which is exactly why it collects nothing. No name, no age, no email address, no login, no way to reach anybody. There is nowhere in Comic Crew for a child to type something that leaves the device: the words in a speech balloon stay on the paper and in this browser, with the rest of the drawing.

Because no personal information is collected from anyone, of any age, there is nothing for a parent to ask us to hand over, correct or delete, and no consent for us to ask for. The section above about the hosting log is the one thing a visit does produce, and you now know as much about it as we do. If you would like to check the rest for yourself, here is how.

How to check any of this yourself

Turn off Wi-Fi and mobile data, then use the whole app: draw, dress up, pose, add balloons, make the page, print it. All of it works, because the entire app is stored on the device. An app that runs with the network off is not sending drawings anywhere.

If you would rather look directly, open your browser's developer tools, go to the Network tab and use Comic Crew. Loading the page fetches the app's own files from the host, and that is the whole list: after that you will see no requests at all, however long a child draws for. Under Application, then Local Storage, you can read the one thing it stores, comic-crew.character, and delete it there.

Tips

Comic Crew is free and stays free. If a Leave a tip link is present in the Grown ups panel, tapping it opens the payment provider's own page in a new tab, on their site. We never see or store card details, tipping unlocks nothing because nothing is locked, and it sits behind the Grown ups panel rather than anywhere a child drawing a comic will meet it.

Accessibility

The colours on this page were measured, not judged by eye. Body text sits at 16.5:1 against the paper, quieter text at 6.8:1 and links at 6.2:1. After dark those become 15.8:1, 8.1:1 and 8.1:1. WCAG 2.1 AA asks for 4.5:1. The page is one column with real headings in order, a skip link, and no sideways scrolling at 320 pixels wide.

In the app itself every control is at least 44 pixels, the focus ring is process magenta and always visible, reduced motion is respected, and the drawing surface works with a finger, a stylus or a mouse.
