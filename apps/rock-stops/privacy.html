Privacy & Accessibility

Rock Stops is a field log for rockhounding, fossils and sea glass, made by Sky Wolf Studios. Rockhounding spots are the thing people guard hardest, so the short version is the whole version: your finds, your photos and your coordinates are on your device and have never been anywhere else.

Where your finds go

Nowhere. Every photo, label, note, site, trip and GPS coordinate is written to this browser's own IndexedDB storage on this device. There is no account, no sign-in, no server to sync to, and no copy held anywhere we can reach. We could not hand your spots to anyone if we were asked for them, because we do not have them.

The part people usually get wrong

The map does not download map tiles, and that is deliberate. The usual way to build a map is to request image tiles for the rectangle you are looking at. That means the moment you open your map, a third-party tile server receives your IP address alongside the precise coordinates of every place you have found anything — which would make “your spots never leave your device” false at exactly the moment you looked at them.

So Float draws the map itself, on your phone, from your own points. You lose roads and terrain. You keep the map of your spots being nobody else's business.

How to check that for yourself

Turn off Wi-Fi and mobile data, then use the app: photograph a find, name it, open the map, print or export. All of it works, because the whole app is stored on your device by its service worker — and an app that runs with the network off is an app that is not sending your locations anywhere.

If you would rather look directly, open your browser's developer tools, go to the Network tab and use Float. You will see no requests at all while you work. Under Application → IndexedDB you can see exactly what is stored, and delete it whenever you like.

Sharing is the one thing that leaves, and only when you do it

  • Share a find sends the photo and what you called it, through your own phone's share sheet, to whoever you pick. It deliberately does not include coordinates — showing a friend a rock should never quietly hand over the place it came from.
  • Export a backup writes a .json file, photos included, to your own downloads. It is not uploaded. It is also the only copy that exists off this device, so make one regularly.
  • Export CSV writes a spreadsheet of labels, materials, sites, zones, dates and coordinates — again to your own downloads only. This one does contain coordinates, because it is your record; treat the file the way you would treat a notebook with your spots written in it.

What we do not do

  • No account, no sign-in, no email address collected.
  • No ads, no analytics, no trackers, no cookies, no fingerprinting.
  • No cloud sync, no community feed, no leaderboard, no sharing of spots with anyone.
  • No third-party scripts and no map tiles — Float makes no network requests in normal use.
  • No selling or brokering of anything. There is no data here to sell.

Permissions

Camera — only in Field mode, and only to show you the viewfinder and take the photo you asked for. The image is processed on your device and stored there.

Location — optional, and only while Field mode is open or when you tap “use my location” on a site. It stamps the find and places the pin. It is never transmitted, and the app works without it.

Your browser asks you for both, not us, and you can refuse either. Float keeps working — you can add photos from your library and set coordinates by typing them.

Storage, and the honest warning that comes with it

Local-only means the backup problem is yours. After your first find, Float asks the browser to mark its storage as persistent, which stops it being cleared automatically when the device is short of space. That is a request, not a guarantee. Clearing site data, uninstalling the app, or a browser cleaning house will take your collection with it. Export a backup regularly.

Hosting

Float's files are served by Google's Firebase Hosting, which — like any web host — keeps standard, short-lived server logs such as IP addresses. That records that a browser fetched the page. It cannot record anything about your finds, because your finds are never sent to it.

Children

Float is made for adults and older kids who go out looking for rocks. It is not directed at children, and it collects no personal information from anyone, of any age.

Accessibility

Every text colour is checked against its background to WCAG 2.1 AA (4.5:1), in both the light and dark parts of the app, and that check runs in the test suite so it cannot quietly regress. Tap targets are at least 44–46px, focus is always visible, reduced-motion is respected, and the field-mode controls are sized and placed for one thumb in bad light.

Changes
