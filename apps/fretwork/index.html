Note hunt

Where is every C? Tap all of them. The drill a teacher assigns first, because it works.

Name that fret

A note lights up, you name it. The reverse direction, so recall works both ways.

Intervals

From a gold root, tap the interval up. Chords and lines are built from these.

Triads

Build the shape on a string set: root position, 1st or 2nd inversion, spelled honestly.

Sevenths, drop 2

The jazz comping workhorse: four notes, four inversions, one string set at a time.

Inversion climb

Build a seventh chord down low, then move every voice up to its next chord tone. The root climbs to the 3rd, the 7th to the root, the 3rd to the 5th, the 5th to the 7th. Four shapes, bottom to top.

The one note ladder

Start on maj7. Flat the 7th and it is a 7. Flat the 3rd for m7. Flat the 5th for m7♭5. Flat the 7th again for dim7. Five chords, one note at a time, same spot on the neck.

Setup

Numbers first, on purpose: a note without a job is trivia. R, 3, 5 and 7 are what the note is doing, and that is what transfers to every key and every chart.

Modes, one altered note at a time

 

The system: learn two scales, major and minor. Every mode is one of them with one note bent. The gold notes on the board are the bent ones: that is the sound you are chasing. Park on a mode, run the vamp, and make the gold note land on purpose.

Diatonically the majors live at 1, 4 and 5 (the 5 turns dominant), the minors at 2, 3 and 6, and 7 carries two altered tones. Same seven notes, seven doors in.

Free board. Tap anything, hear it, see its name.
